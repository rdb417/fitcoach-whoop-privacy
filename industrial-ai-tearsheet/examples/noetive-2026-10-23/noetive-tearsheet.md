## A. Company and product
Agents run on top of a customer's existing systems and make operational decisions end to end, fed by a multimodal sensing pod Noetive designs [2] *(CR)*. The company says design partners in manufacturing, logistics, energy and data centers use it [2] *(CR)*.

## B. Technology and differentiation
A core model (the "brain") learns how an operation runs from system data and pod signals, acts, and learns from outcomes; each deployment is meant to improve the shared model [2,3] *(CR)*.

- **Not disclosed:** in-house model or agents on frontier models; what the pod senses; write-back to ERP or advice only *(Unknown)*.
- **Likely moat, if any:** plan-versus-actual data compounding across sites, not the model *(INF)*. The pod adds data rivals lack, but also hardware cost and, in German plants, works-council approval for monitoring devices *(INF)*.

## C. Market and competition
Wedge appears to be production planning in process plants, today run in ERP or APS tools, spreadsheets and planner judgment *(INF)*.

- **Palantir AIP / Warp Speed:** scheduling and production workflows over ERP, MES and PLM data, sold to manufacturers [6].
- **Archetype AI:** sensor-data foundation model (Newton); $35M round Nov 2025 [5]. Closest on physical sensing.
- **SAP, Kinaxis, o9:** the planning suites Noetive sits on, likely to add agents themselves *(INF)*.

## D. Team and financing
CEO Amir Frenkel spent nearly a decade as a Meta VP over AI applied research, after Google (Glass), Amazon, TI and Palm [4]. CPO Dan Barak founded Stackbit, sold to Netlify, after product roles at Lyft and Meta [3]. Eclipse led the $41M seed [1], joined by 19 others including Craft, Westly, Gigascale and angels Bosworth and Schroepfer [1]. No founder with plant operations background disclosed *(Unknown)*.

## E. Commercial traction
One named partner: Steuben Foods, an aseptic food and beverage manufacturer, whose CEO Menachem Katz says planning that ran monthly over a week now runs daily in minutes [2]. A design-partner quote, not a measured result; paid status and vendor effort undisclosed *(Unknown)*. PitchBook tags it as generating revenue, no figures [1].

## F. Preliminary investment perspective
**What is interesting.** Frontier-AI team aimed at operational decisions, with a customer CEO describing a step change within months. The bet: sensing plus outcome data compounds into a model incumbents cannot copy *(INF)*.

**What needs validation.** Product versus forward-deployed engineering per site; and Eclipse dependence (it built the company, led the round and employs the CEO): board, terms, room for an outside lead.

**Leitmotif relevance.** Fits the industrial AI thesis; no portfolio overlap found. Planning and inbound logistics at Volkswagen Group component or PowerCo plants are a plausible later test *(INF)*.

## G. Three questions for the introductory call
1. **At Steuben, what does the system decide versus recommend, which systems does it write back to, and how many Noetive engineer-days a week does it still take?** *Separates a product from forward-deployed services.*
2. **What does the pod capture that ERP and MES data missed, and how much of the Steuben gain remains without it?** *Tests whether the hardware is the moat or a deployment drag.*
3. **What did the brain learn at one design partner that made the next deployment faster, and how do you measure that?** *Tests the cross-customer learning claim the thesis rests on.*
